What happens when the call goes unanswered?

The caller does not wait. Invoca reported on May 23, 2024 that its research found 27% of calls to home services businesses were not answered, and that its platform data showed less than 3% of callers sent to voicemail left a message. Those are Invoca’s figures, not Alln1 customers.

That is the leak. You are doing the job they called about, so you cannot pick up, and they hire the next company on the list. Pew Research Center’s 2026 mobile fact sheet says 98% of U.S. adults own a cellphone and 91% own a smartphone, from a survey run January 30 to June 18, 2026. A call can still reach almost everyone. AT&T’s Connection Quiz, updated May 1, 2024, said 60% of quiz takers preferred a text over a call. That is a quiz, not a random sample, so many of your callers will still want a voice. How does the receptionist work?

Five steps. You approve the words before any of them talk to a real customer. Nothing on this website is a live line you can call today.

  1. The trigger. Someone dials the number already on your truck and your Google listing.
  2. The information it uses. Your services, your hours, your cities, and the sentences you approved. It does not search the web.
  3. What it does. It says it is an assistant, asks the name, address, and what is wrong, then books an opening, takes a message, or rings you.
  4. What the caller gets. A clear next step, or your voice if the words match your transfer list. They are not left with a beep.
  5. Where you see it. A recap in email or in the job software you already open. No new program.

What can you turn on, and what happens if it fails?

You pick the rules before launch, and you can change them later. If something breaks, calls come back to your phone instead of vanishing.

  • Spanish. Off until you want it. You approve those sentences the same way you approve the English. Other languages are on the more languages page.
  • After hours only, or all day. The closed greeting can take a message or offer the next open morning. It will not promise a truck tonight unless you wrote that sentence.
  • Booking. It can offer only openings you already published, or it can hold the details until you tap yes. If the calendar has no opening, it takes a message.
  • Routing. You write the transfer words. Gas smell, water that will not shut off, water coming through a ceiling. Those ring your cell. A filter change does not, unless you add it.
  • Approvals. A price, a promise to roll a truck, and anything about a tenant stay with a person. The assistant says “a person will confirm.”
  • When it fails. If the forward drops, the call rings your phone as it does today. If the assistant is unsure, it does not guess. It takes the name and emails you. You can turn the forward off the same day.

Who is this for, and who should skip it?

It fits a shop where the owner or the tech is the person who would have answered. It is a poor fit if a person already sits on the phone all day and you want every call to stay with that person.

Good fit for:

  • Trades and home services
  • Salons and barbers
  • Property management

It is not a fit for medical advice, legal advice, or a decision about a tenant’s rent or a notice. A person does those. On a property call it can take the unit and whether water is spreading. It cannot screen a resident or change the rent. See property management.

What do owners ask before they trust it?

These are the worries we hear. Short answers first.

Can I keep my number?

Yes. Callers keep dialing the number on the truck, the cards, and Google. We forward that number. You are not asking Houston to learn a new one.

Will customers know it is a computer?

Yes. The greeting says it is an assistant. We will not write a script that pretends a person is in the office if that person is not there.

What if it says the wrong thing?

You hear the test calls before a customer does. After that, the recap shows what was said, and we change the wording. It cannot offer a price you did not write.

Can a real person still get the call?

Yes. Take every call, only the transfer words, or switch the assistant off. If you are under a house and do not pick up, it finishes the message path you approved.

Can I turn it off?

Yes. Stop the forward and calls come back to your phone or to voicemail. We show you that switch. You do not need us on the line to use it.

Will it speak Spanish?

It can, and only if you want it. You approve those sentences. Languages beyond English and Spanish are a separate page.

Will it text the caller?

Only if you ask for that, and only after the carrier approves. Texting is not sending from this website. The voice path does not wait on that approval.

What if my calendar is wrong?

It can only offer openings that are actually on the calendar, or it can wait for your tap. A wrong calendar means a wrong offer, so we test that before launch. If there is no opening, it takes a message.

How is this different from an answering service?

An answering service is another company reading a script and sending you a note. This uses your facts, your openings, and your transfer list, and you hear it before it is real.

Do I have to buy new software?

No. You keep opening the email, Jobber, or Housecall Pro you already check. The audit shows which of them will connect.

Can it quote a price?

No. A price you did not write becomes “a person will confirm.” Photo pricing is a different page, photo-to-quote, and you still set the number.

Can I try it from this page today?

No. We set your line up after the audit and the test calls. This page is not a live receptionist.
