Can AI automate a website that has no API?

Often, yes. A browser agent can open a portal, read the page, fill in fields and download files the way a person does. It's slower than a direct integration and needs attention when a site changes, so we use it where no integration exists and keep an approval step before anything is submitted.

Who it's for

Businesses that retype the same information into supplier, insurance, permit or government portals.

What it connects to

The portal in a browser, Google Sheets or your CRM for the data, email alerts.

What the workflow can look like

  1. A trigger starts the run, such as a new order, a schedule or a request from your team.
  2. The agent opens the portal with access you grant for this purpose.
  3. It reads, fills in, downloads or copies the information the task needs.
  4. Before anything is submitted, paid or sent, it stops for a person to approve.
  5. Results are logged, and you're alerted if the site changes or a step fails.

Illustrative scenario only. This is not a client case study, a live product demo or a promise of specific results.

What stays in your hands

  • You approve every submission, and the agent never pays or signs for you.
  • You control the access and can turn it off any time.
  • We follow each site's terms of use, and some sites don't allow automation.

What we confirm in your AI Audit

  • Which portals take up your team's time.
  • What each task involves, step by step.
  • Each site's terms and sign-in rules.

Questions before you start

Is this the same as RPA?

It's similar to robotic process automation, but AI reads pages that change instead of following fixed clicks, so it breaks less often.

What kinds of portals?

Supplier ordering sites, insurance and benefits portals, permit and licensing sites, and older software with a web screen.

What if the website changes?

The agent alerts you when a step fails, and care plans cover fixing it.

Is it allowed?

It depends on each site's terms. We check them in the AI Audit and skip sites that don't allow automation.
