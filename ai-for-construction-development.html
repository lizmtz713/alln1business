Who this package is for

General contractors, custom and infill home builders, and small residential or multifamily developers running several projects at once, where costs, vendor documents and commitments are spread across email, spreadsheets and accounting. New firms can start with the launch stage; active builders usually start with a one-project operations pilot.

Problems we hear in this industry

You learn the real margin too late

Bills are coded inconsistently or late, so job profitability shows up after the money is spent.

Vendor insurance and licenses expire quietly

Certificates of insurance and license dates live in inboxes until something lapses.

Project meetings create scattered commitments

Decisions and to-dos from site and partner meetings don’t reliably become assigned tasks.

Cash is committed before the full picture is visible

Upcoming bills, draws and payments are hard to see in one place week to week.

Buyer, landowner and partner inquiries lose context

Conversations sit in personal inboxes, and follow-up depends on memory.

Fictional example: a four-home infill project

Picture an imaginary developer building four patio homes on one infill lot. Supplier invoices arrive by email from three vendors. Each one is read, matched to the project and cost code, and placed in a review queue next to the budget line; one bill that doesn’t match its purchase order is flagged instead of entered. A framing subcontractor’s insurance certificate is 21 days from expiring, so a renewal request draft waits for the project manager. Every Monday the owner reads a one-page brief: costs against budget, open vendor documents and commitments from last week’s site meeting.

Made up to show how the pieces connect. Not a real business, client story, testimonial or measured result.

How the connected flow works

  1. Vendor bills and receipts are read and matched to a project and cost code.
  2. Bills wait in a review queue next to the budget; mismatches are flagged.
  3. Insurance certificates and license dates are tracked with renewal drafts.
  4. Meeting notes become assigned tasks with due dates.
  5. A weekly owner brief shows costs, open documents and commitments.
  6. The owner or project manager approves budgets, change orders and every payment.

Questions before you start

Can AI approve bills or pay vendors?

No. AI prepares bills and flags mismatches. The owner or an authorized person approves every bill and payment.

Does this work with my accounting and project tools?

Common accounting tools such as QuickBooks Online are a frequent fit. Project, folder and task tools are checked in the audit before we promise a connection.

Can it track subcontractor insurance?

Yes, a COI and license tracker can watch expiration dates and draft renewal requests. It summarizes documents; it does not certify coverage.

Can it handle permits or inspections?

It can remind you of dates you enter and organize documents. It does not submit permits or make zoning, safety or engineering judgments.

Where should a developer start?

Usually with one active project: vendor bills, job coding, missing documents and a weekly owner brief. Expand once that flow is proven.
