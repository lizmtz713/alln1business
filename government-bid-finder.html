How do small businesses find government contracts?

Most public contracts are posted on bid boards, such as SAM.gov for federal work and state and local procurement sites. A bid finder checks those boards on a schedule, matches new postings to your services and summarizes the requirements, so you spend your time deciding and bidding instead of searching.

Who it's for

What it connects to

SAM.gov and state and local bid boards, Google Sheets, Google Docs, email.

What the workflow can look like

  1. We list the boards that matter: federal, state, county and city, plus any agencies you sell to.
  2. New postings are checked on a schedule, where each board's terms allow it.
  3. AI matches postings to your services, location and size, and skips the rest.
  4. Each match gets a plain summary: scope, deadline, requirements, forms and a bid or no-bid checklist.
  5. For bids you pursue, AI drafts answers to standard questions and a compliance checklist for you to finish.

Illustrative scenario only. This is not a client case study, a live product demo or a promise of specific results.

Questions before you start

Which bid boards can it watch?

Federal postings on SAM.gov and state and local boards, as each board's terms allow. We confirm your list in the audit.

Can it write the whole proposal?

It drafts standard sections and answers from your past material. You review, price and submit.

Does it help with minority or women-owned business certification?

It can keep a checklist of the documents those programs ask for. You apply with the certifying agency.

How soon will we win a contract?

No one can promise that. Public contracts often take months from posting to award.
